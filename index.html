A three-part visualisation

Following Summer

Most migratory birds are not fleeing the cold. They are following daylight — and the food that daylight grows. These three pieces take the same six-to-eight lineages and ask three different questions of them: who migrates, where they go, and when, and why then.

Vol. I — Who

Canopy of Migration

The bird family tree as a rotating 3D canopy, from the flightless ratites at one edge to the Arctic Tern at the other. Colour runs from sedentary to extreme-endurance; branch thickness follows species count.

Drag to orbit · click a clade for its strategy Open Vol. I → Vol. II — Where

Flyway Atlas

Six species plotted on a world map, each moving along its own flyway as you scrub through the calendar — a radar-style read on where every population sits today.

Scrub the year · isolate a single flyway Open Vol. II → Vol. III — When

Chasing the Sun

The same six routes as true great circles on a WebGL globe, lit by a day/night terminator that moves with the date — so you can watch daylight pool at one pole, then the other.

Spin the earth · play the year · read daylight hours Open Vol. III →

About the data

These are modelled illustrations built from published satellite-tracking and range studies — not a live radar or telemetry feed.

Breeding and wintering locations, route shapes, and migration windows follow the published literature for each species; positions between those anchor points are interpolated, not observed. Daylight hours are computed from real solar declination for the selected date, so those figures are genuine astronomy.

For live nocturnal migration radar over North America, see BirdCast; for observation records, eBird.

How it is built

Three self-contained HTML pages, no build step and no framework. Vol. I and II render to 2D canvas with hand-rolled projection; Vol. III uses Three.js with a custom GLSL shader for the terminator, and GSAP for camera flights and the year timeline.

The globe's earth texture is painted at runtime from coastline outlines rather than loaded as an image, so the whole site ships without a single binary asset.

Everything works from a plain static host. Drop the folder anywhere that serves files.
